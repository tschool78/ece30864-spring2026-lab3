1. By using AGILE development, you are able to rapidly adapte to changing situations. You can change the requirements mid project. However, since there is no final set of requirements, the project can grow in complexity throughout the project. Non-AGILE devlopment is very predictable from the strart. But, since the fluidity is lacking, you are unable to see a working product until the very and where major flaws could be seen.

2. The product owner is the voice of the customer and the business. They are responsible for defining "done", stakeholder management, and creater the list of tasks. The Scrum Master is the leader in the JIRA space. They are in charge of running stand-ups, sprint planning, and ensureing that the team follows scrum values. 

3. The first meeting is Spring Planning. At the very beginning of the sprint to set Sprint Goals, they are used to discuse the blacklog where the product owner presents the highest priority items. Also, the team breaks down the selected items to specific techincal tasks. Next are the Daily Stan-ups. In these 15 minutes meetings, progress is discussed to keep the team moving. The team can also identify any blocking impediments. Finally is the spring retrospective. In this meeting, the teams get together at the end up the spring and discuss process improvement for the next spring, and identify action items to implement the increased efficiency. 

4. An epic is a large body of work that can be broken down into smaller story pieces. It should take multiple sprints to complete a epic. A user story is a small, functional requirement that should be small enough to be completed within a single sprint. A bug is a problem or defect that needs to be fixed before a story can be called complete. A task is a techincal work that the developer need to do to keep a project moving. An example would be specific feature to implement a story item.
